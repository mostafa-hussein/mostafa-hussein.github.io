Teaching a Robot to Write in English and Arabic

Learning perceptual and motion primitives from demonstration.

Assiut University, Egypt · 2013 – 2017 · M.Sc. research

Most learning-from-demonstration methods assume each demonstration arrives neatly separated. A robot watching people instead sees a continuous stream that mixes intended and incidental demonstrations.

COLD (ICMA 2017) is a modular, robot-independent ROS framework that learns from such continuous streams: motif discovery finds the repeated actions (here, strokes and letters) inside them. The motion primitives are Gaussian mixture models whose number of components is chosen by variational Bayesian inference (IEA/AIE 2015), rather than by costly model selection.

By watching people write, the robot learned the basic strokes of English and Arabic, two scripts with very different stroke structure, and then wrote new words that never appeared in the demonstrations, on a physical NAO and a parallel-robot simulator.

Published at ICMA 2017 and IEA/AIE 2015 (Hussein et al., 2017; Hussein et al., 2015).

Tools: ROS · Gaussian mixture models · variational Bayes · motif discovery · NAO

2017

  1. ICMA
    COLD: A ROS package for continuous Learning from Demonstration teaching a robot to write
    Mostafa Hussein, Yasser Mohammad, and Samia A. Ali
    In 2017 IEEE International Conference on Mechatronics and Automation (ICMA), 2017
    Abs DOI Bib

    Learning From Demonstration (LfD) is an important area of research in robotics because it provides the means for skill transfer from humans to robots with no reliance on any specific technical skills of the human teacher. There are many approaches to LfD. Most available methods assume the existence of clear boundaries between different demonstrations. Less research focuses on techniques that can be used by the robot to learn from continuous streams of data that provide not only planned but also unplanned demonstrations. This paper reports an integrated framework to tackle this problem using the Robot Operating System (ROS) that is modular, scalable, easily adaptable, and robot-independent. We also report a study applying this method to teach robots how to write in both English and Arabic by just watching people writing short reports. Not only can the learner write words that exist in the training data but novel words based on the basic strokes learned from the demonstrations. The system was applied to teaching both a physical NAO robot and a Parallel robot simulator and evaluations demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in this task.

2015

  1. IEA/AIE
    Learning from Demonstration Using Variational Bayesian Inference
    Mostafa Hussein, Yasser Mohammad, and Samia A. Ali
    In Current Approaches in Applied Artificial Intelligence: 28th International Conference on Industrial, Engineering and Other Applications of Applied Intelligent Systems (IEA/AIE 2015), 2015
    Abs DOI Bib

    Learning from demonstration (LFD) is an active area of research in robotics. There are many approaches to LFD. One of the most widely used approaches is the combination of Gaussian Mixture Model learning for modeling and Gaussian Mixture Regression for behavior generation (GMM/GMR) due to its advantages including easy learning using Expectation Maximization and the simplicity of serializing learned behaviors as well as the ability to model internal correlations and constraints within the task. A critical parameter that affects the accuracy of learned behavior in GMM/GMR is the number of components in the mixture. A handful of approaches for selecting this number can be found in the literature including classical model selection methods like Bayesian Information Criteria and Akaike Information Criteria and more advanced methods including Dirichlet Process modeling. These approaches are either wasteful of computational resources or hard to implement. This paper introduces a LfD approach which uses GMM with a variational Bayesian Inference (VB) approach to select the number of Gaussians that best fit the data. The proposed method is compared to classical model selection approaches and a recently proposed symbolization based method and is shown to provide an appropriate balance between execution speed and model accuracy.
